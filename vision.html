Our vision · Military-connected children

Learning that moves with the child.

Children in military families pack up their lives again and again. We believe their education shouldn't have to start over every time they do.

The challenge

When a family receives new orders, the children move too. Families typically move every two to three years, and each move can mean new teachers, new expectations, and a new set of friends to make. Even where programs share a curriculum, a child's individual progress rarely travels with them. Some children land a step ahead. Others land a step behind. Almost all of them have to rebuild their sense of belonging from scratch.

These are some of the most resilient children in the country. We think the systems around them can do more to make their resilience easier.

The idea

Montessori education is built around the individual child. Each child moves through the curriculum at their own pace, in mixed-age classrooms organized around three-year stages of development. That structure travels well: the materials, the environment, and the expectations look the same from one Montessori classroom to the next.

We imagine on-base schools and child development centers where Montessori practice lets each child's individual progress travel with them, so a child who transfers picks up where they left off instead of starting over.

Continuity

Each child's individual progress travels with them, so learning follows the child instead of the address. No more repeating lessons, or missing them.

Belonging

Mixed-age, three-year classroom communities where older children mentor younger ones and newcomers find their place quickly, in environments that nurture children emotionally as well as academically.

Alignment

A longer-term question worth exploring: could the timing of family moves one day line up with natural three-year developmental cycles, so more children complete a full cycle in one community?

Why it matters

Children who feel settled are free to learn. Families who trust the next school can focus on the mission in front of them. And children who grow up with a positive experience of military life may be more likely to see it as a path for their own future.

Where we are today: this is an early-stage vision. We are developing the research behind it and drawing on our experience operating Montessori programs. We are looking for people who want to help shape what comes next.

Help shape the vision

We are inviting early conversations with:

  • Philanthropists and foundations who care about military families and want to fund a model that could scale.
  • Military-connected educators and families who have lived these transitions and know what children need.
  • Researchers and policy leaders working on child development, education continuity, and family readiness.
